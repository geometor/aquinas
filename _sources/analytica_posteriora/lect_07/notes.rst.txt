Commentary & Notes — That No Particular Science Demonstrates Its Own Proper Principles, Which Belong to First Philosophy
========================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Commenting on *Posterior Analytics* I.9 (75b37–76a30), Aquinas integrates :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's prohibition of kind-crossing (*metabasis eis allo genos*, established in I.7) with the hierarchy of subalternated sciences (*harmonics* under arithmetic, *optics* and *mechanics* under geometry) and the architectural supremacy of *first philosophy* (*Metaphysics* IV.1–3, VI.1). Relying on the ancient commentary tradition (:person:`Themistius <themistius>`, Philoponus, and pseudo-Philoponus preserved in medieval scholia on *Sophistical Refutations* 11 and *Posterior Analytics* I.9), Aquinas reconstructs Bryson of Heraclea's attempted quadrature of the circle to show why a syllogism built solely from common notions (*greater, less, and equal*) lacks a proximate middle term *per se* proportioned to circular curvature.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Bryson's fallacy illuminates the profound methodological rigor of Book XII of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements* (derived from Eudoxus of Cnidus) and Archimedes' *Measurement of a Circle*. Unlike Bryson, who leaped illicitly from inscribed and circumscribed squares to an equal square via a purely dialectical appeal to *greater and less*, Eudoxus and Euclid in *Elements* XII.2 (grounded in *Elements* X.1, the Eudoxian-Archimedean exhaustion lemma) remain strictly within the *proper principles of continuous magnitude*, proving area proportionality through rigorous geometric bisection of arcs and polygons. Meanwhile, Euclid's *Optics* and *Sectio Canonis* exemplify the exact subalternation of visual perspective to geometry and musical intervals to arithmetic described in paragraphs 3 and 6.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

:person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` cites *In I Anal. Post.*, lect. 17, no. 4 in Chapter I of *De Noetica Geometriae* (and develops its consequences across Chapters V–VII) to establish the proper boundary between geometry and metaphysics. On the one hand, against logicism (Frege and :person:`Russell <russell>`\ 's *Principia Mathematica*), Lecture 17 proves that particular mathematical sciences cannot be deduced from purely general logical or ontological axioms alone without *proper quantitative principles* abstracted from extension—otherwise all mathematics degenerates into Brysonian *metabasis*. On the other hand, because geometry cannot demonstrate its own first principles within geometry, critical reflection on their ultimate epistemic validity belongs rightfully to noetic metaphysics.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Both halves of Lecture 17 resonate directly with GEOMETOR. First, Bryson's continuity heuristic—sandwiching a circle between smaller and larger squares without a constructive geometric middle—foreshadows numeric root-bracketing approximations, whereas *geometor.model* insists on *proper* compass-and-straightedge algebraic intersections (precisely distinguishing constructible quadratic extensions from transcendental circle-squaring). Second, Aquinas's account of subalternation—where geometry supplies the *propter quid* formal causes for *optics*, *mechanics*, and *harmonics*—animates GEOMETOR's cross-disciplinary reach into botanical *phyllotaxis* (*geometor.phyllotaxis*) and harmonic proportion.
