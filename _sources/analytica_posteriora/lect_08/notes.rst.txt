Commentary & Notes — On the Three Elements Required in Every Demonstrative Science: Subject-Genus, Proper Attributes, and Common Axioms
=======================================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Expositing *Posterior Analytics* I.10 (76a31–b22), Aquinas systematizes :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's canonical triad of every demonstrative science (*omnis scientia demonstrativa est circa tria*: *genus subiectum*, *communes dignitates*, *passiones*). Particularly significant is Aquinas's explanation of how common axioms enter particular sciences *according to analogy* (*kat' analogian*, 76a38): transcendental or trans-generic relations of equality are proportionally contracted to discrete number in arithmetic and continuous magnitude in geometry. Notice too how Aquinas expands Aristotle's brief geometric examples (*euthy*, *keklasmenon*) by introducing technical Euclidean-Arabic terminology from Campanus of Novara and Adelard of Bath—defining *rational* (*commensurable*) versus *irrational* or *surd* (*surda*) lines.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Lecture 18 reads like a structural blueprint of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements*. First, the *subject-genus* comprises continuous magnitude in Books I–VI and XI–XIII (points and lines, *signa*) and discrete units/numbers in Books VII–IX. Second, the *common axioms* contracted *secundum analogiam* appear verbatim in Euclid's Common Notion 3 (*If equals be subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal*), which Euclid applies to geometric segments and areas in Book I (e.g., I.2, I.3, I.35) and to numbers in Book VII. Third, the *proper attributes* whose nominal definitions are posited and whose existence is demonstrated span Books I, VII–IX (even, odd, square, and cube numbers, VII Defs. 6–7, 18–19), and Book X Def. 3 (lines *rational* [*rhetai*] and *irrational* [*alogoi*, medieval *surdae*] relative to an assigned reference line).

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In Chapter V and Chapter VII of *De Noetica Geometriae* (linking *In I Anal. Post.*, lect. 15 and 18 with *Metaphysics* V and X), :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` examines how common axioms such as *subtracting equals from equals* function *analogically* across heterogeneous genera of quantity. Against formalist axiomatics that flatten equality into a bare syntactic equivalence relation, Hoenen emphasizes with Aquinas that equality in continuous extension (congruence and measure) and equality in discrete multitude are proportionally distinct realizations grounded in their respective *subject-genera*, and that Book X's theory of *rational* and *surd* lines arises precisely when continuous magnitude transcends discrete numerical ratios.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's highlight of :person:`Euclid <euclid>` Book X's *rational* versus *irrational* (*surd*) lines relative to a *given reference line* (*linea data*) directly describes the foundational coordinate and symbolic architecture of *geometor.model* and *geometor.divine*. By fixing two initial points at (0, 0) and (1, 0) as the *linea data*, *geometor.model* computes every constructible segment length as an exact SymPy expression—distinguishing rational lengths from quadratic *surds* such as √2, √3, and φ—while applying common algebraic axioms analogically to verify exact segment, area, and cross-ratio equalities across the construction.
