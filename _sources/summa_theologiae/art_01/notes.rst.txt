Commentary & Notes — Being Itself as Most Formal of All; the Geometer's Infinite Line; Apprehending Absolute Being
==================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

In *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 7, a. 1 and 3, Aquinas transforms :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's critique of the actual infinite (*Physics* III.4–8 and *De Caelo* I.5–7) by synthesizing it with Neoplatonic-Christian participation metaphysics (John Damascene and Pseudo-Dionysius). For the Presocratics and even for Aristotle, *to apeiron* carried the connotation of unfinished material indeterminacy—potency lacking terminating *peras* or form. Aquinas accepts Aristotle's hylomorphic premise for continuous magnitude—proving from natural place, diverging radii in circular rotation (*De Caelo* I.5, 271b26–272a7), and quantitative figure that no physical or mathematical body can be actually infinite—while inverting its metaphysical implication for pure form and *esse*: because form is contracted rather than perfected by matter, and *ipsum esse* is most formal of all (*maxime formale omnium*), subsistent *esse* unreceived in any limiting potency is supremely infinite and perfect.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Three foundational Euclidean doctrines converge in Question 7, Articles 1 and 3. First, Aquinas's principle that *figura* is the quantitative form consisting in the termination of magnitude (*q. 7, a. 1, ad 2* and *a. 3, co.*) directly quotes :person:`Euclid <euclid>` *Elements* Book I, Definition 14: *Figura est quae termino vel terminis continetur* ('A figure is that which is contained by any boundary or boundaries'). Second, Objection 1 and Reply 1 of Article 3 resolve a classic puzzle concerning Euclid's Postulate 2 ('To produce a finite straight line continuously in a straight line') and propositions such as *Elements* I.12 ('upon a given infinite straight line, from a given point which is not on it, to draw a perpendicular straight line') and I.22: following :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` (*Physics* III.7, 207b29–31), Aquinas explains that the geometer never assumes an actually infinite line, but calls a finite line 'infinite' (*indefinite*) insofar as it may be extended or cut off as far as any given construction requires. Third, Aquinas's geometric proof against infinite rotation (*a. 3, co.*) invokes the unbounded divergence of two rays drawn at an angle from a center.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

:person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` explicitly cites *ST* I, q. 7, a. 1 (*illud quod est maxime formale omnium, est ipsum esse*, concordance key *aquinas:st:I.7.1*) and *ST* I, q. 12, a. 4, ad 3 (*intellectus noster potest in abstractione considerare id quod in concretione cognoscit*, concordance key *aquinas:st:I.12.4.ad3*) in the Appendix to *De Noetica Geometriae* (nn. 307, 410). For Hoenen, Question 12, Article 4, ad 3 supplies the decisive proof that human intelligence possesses a genuine, abstractive conception (*ratio essendi*) of *esse* itself—not merely concrete quiddities—through a 'certain resolution' (*per modum resolutionis cuiusdam*) analogous to its resolution of form from matter. Because the intellect resolves concrete beings into essence and existential act, it can formulate universal existential principles and recognize *esse* as the most formal perfection grounding every categorical mode of being, including quantitative extension.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's resolution of the *linea infinita geometrae* in *ST* I, q. 7, a. 3, ad 1 describes with uncanny exactitude how unbounded lines operate in *geometor.model* and *geometor.explorer*. In *geometor.model*, a *Line* constructed through two points is represented algebraically by homogeneous linear coefficients (*a*, *b*, *c*) satisfying *ax + by + c = 0*—not as a completed actual infinity of points, but as an indefinite rule of alignment capable of yielding exact finite intersections wherever another line or circle meets it, while *geometor.explorer* clips and renders that line across whatever finite bounding viewport the current construction demands. At the same time, Aquinas's theorem that every actual geometric figure is bounded by terminating limits (*figura*) underpins the finite, exact constructibility of polygons and polyhedra studied in *geometor.elements* and *geometor.pacioli*.
