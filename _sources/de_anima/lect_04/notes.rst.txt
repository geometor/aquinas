Commentary & Notes — Why the Soul Never Understands Without a Phantasm and How Mathematical Entities Are Understood in Abstraction
==================================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Aquinas expounds *De Anima* III.7 (431a1–b19), a dense dossier of Aristotelian insights uniting cognitive psychology, theory of action, and philosophy of mathematics. Beginning from the crucial distinction at 431a4–7 between physical *kinesis* (incomplete act of the imperfect) and cognitive *energeia* or *operatio* (complete act of the perfected power, nos. 764–765), Aquinas traces :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's analogy between sensory discrimination and intellectual judgment (431a8–b2), culminating in the celebrated dictum at 431a16–17: διὸ οὐδέποτε νοεῖ ἄνευ φαντάσματος ἡ ψυχή ('wherefore the soul never understands without a :term:`phantasm <phantasma>`,' no. 771). Aquinas then unpacks 431b12–19 on τὰ ἐν ἀφαιρέσει λεγόμενα ('things spoken of in abstraction,' nos. 780–783), showing how Aristotle replaces Platonic *chorismos* (ontological separation) with selective attention of the :term:`agent intellect <intellectus-agens>`, which contemplates *the curved* without flesh while never falsely judging curvature to subsist apart from bodies.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Two explicit mathematical structures govern Aquinas's commentary in Lecture 12. First, to explain how a single indivisible cognitive power discriminates heterogeneous or contrary qualities without splitting into separate subjects, Aquinas twice invokes the Euclidean circle (Book I, Def. 15): both the common sense and the judging intellect stand as a single unextended center at which multiple radial lines from the external senses and :term:`phantasms <phantasma>` terminate simultaneously (nos. 767, 772). Second, in paragraph 775, Aquinas formalizes :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's lettered proportion at 431a25–29 using Book V, Proposition 16 of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements* (*permutatio proportionum* or *alternando*): if sensible *a* (white) stands to sensible *b* (black) as phantasm *c* stands to phantasm *d*, then by alternation *a* stands to *c* as *b* stands to *d*, proving rigorously that one unified intellect judges *c* and *d* just as one unified sense judges *a* and *b*. Finally, nos. 780–782 ground the legitimacy of Euclidean geometry's idealized curves and surfaces: abstracting *the curved* from sensible flesh involves no mendacity because sensible qualities fall outside curvature's formal ratio.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In *De Noetica Geometriae*, Chapter I, § 2 (explicitly citing *De Anima* III.7, 431a14–b2 and Aquinas, Lecture 12, Pirotta nos. 770–772 and 777 alongside *Posterior Analytics* I.12, 77b30) and Chapter III, :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` demonstrates that mathematical abstraction is an *intuition conjoined with abstraction* occurring *within* the :term:`phantasm <phantasma>`: ταῦτα δ' ἐστὶν οἷον ὁρᾶν τῇ νοήσει ('these mathematicals are, as it were, seen by intellection'). Confronting the sensory 'threshold of exactitude' (*limen exactitudinis*)—the fact that neither eye nor imagination can distinguish a true breadthless line or exact concurrency of three medians from a slight deviation—Hoenen shows from Lecture 12 that the intellect does not merely copy blurry phantasms (Mill's empiricism) nor fabricate axioms *a priori* apart from experience (modern formalism). Instead, inspecting the concrete *phantasm*, the :term:`agent intellect <intellectus-agens>` abstracts formal spatial relations apart from sensible defects (nos. 780–783) and intuits exact, necessary nexus directly in the imagined configuration.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Lecture 12 directly addresses the central epistemological paradox of computational geometry: why visual rendering remains indispensable even when symbolic algebra possesses infinite exactitude. Human perception and screen displays are bounded by :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>`\ 's *limen exactitudinis*—finite SVG stroke widths and floating-point viewport coordinates in ``geometor.explorer`` are sensible approximations—yet 'the soul cannot understand without a :term:`phantasm <phantasma>`\ ' (no. 771). In GEOMETOR, ``geometor.explorer`` supplies the luminous, structured *phantasm* in which the geometer visually deliberates and spots candidate collinearities, concurrencies, and golden sections 'as though actually seeing them' (no. 777), while ``geometor.model`` performs the precise non-mendacious abstraction of nos. 780–783: stripping away every pixel approximation to verify in pure symbolic algebra whether curvature, straightness, and harmonic proportion hold *per se*.
