Commentary & Notes — Whether Divine Science Treats of What Exists Without Matter and Motion
===========================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

In reconciling :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's two characterizations of first philosophy—as the universal science of *being insofar as it is being* (*Metaphysics* IV.1, 1003a21) and as *theology* studying immobile, separable divine substance (*Metaphysics* VI.1, 1026a19; XII.6–10)—Aquinas resolves a celebrated tension debated by Alexander of Aphrodisias, :person:`Avicenna <avicenna>` (*Liber de philosophia prima* I.1–2), and :person:`Averroes <averroes>` (*In I Phys.*, comm. 83). Avicenna had rightly insisted against Averroes that God cannot be the presupposed *subject* of metaphysics because God's existence is proved within metaphysics, not assumed at its outset; Aquinas adopts Avicenna's subject (*ens in quantum est ens*) while showing that God and the separate Intelligences enter metaphysics precisely as the universal causal principles of all being, thereby distinguishing philosophical metaphysics from Scriptural *sacra doctrina* (where divine realities self-revealed in faith constitute the proper subject itself) and refuting Avicebron's universal hylomorphism via :person:`Boethius <boethius>`\ 's *De Hebdomadibus* distinction of *quod est* and *esse*.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Two vital mathematical contrasts sharpen Aquinas's metaphysical doctrine in Article 4. First, in *co. 1*, Aquinas contrasts subsistent causal principles with incomplete intrinsic principles such as *the unit of number and the point of a line* (*unitas numeri et punctus lineae*, corresponding to :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements* VII, Def. 1 and I, Def. 1): because point and unit are not complete natures subsisting apart from magnitude and multitude, they do not generate a separate science prior to geometry and arithmetic, but are treated solely within geometry and arithmetic as principles of their :term:`subject-genus <genus-subiectum>`. Second, in *arg. 7* and *ad 7* (following *Metaphysics* III.2, 996a21–35, where Aristippus mocked mathematics for ignoring the good and final causes), Aquinas explains why Euclidean demonstrations never argue from efficient or final causality: because mathematical figures are separated solely in consideration (*secundum considerationem tantum*), dynamic agency and teleology belong not to abstract figures as such, but only to real subsistent beings.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In the extensive Appendix to *De Noetica Geometriae* (1954) and Chapter X of *La théorie du jugement*, :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` explores how Thomistic epistemology ascends from the quidditative nexus of geometric essences to the metaphysical affirmation of *ipsum esse* and transcendental modes of being. Analyzing Aquinas's distinction in *q. 5, a. 4, co. 4* and *ad 5* between mathematical abstraction (whose objects can never exist apart from matter) and metaphysical *separatio* (which grasps *ens*, *substantia*, *potentia*, and *actus* as neutrally independent of matter *secundum esse*), Hoenen argues against rationalist essentialism—citing Gilson alongside Aquinas's *De Potentia* q. 7, a. 2, ad 9—that *ipsum esse* (*quo est* in *ad 4*) is the actuality of all acts, initially encountered in concrete sensible and kinetic judgments and purified through negative *separatio* without ever turning common being into a univocal quantitative genus.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's precision in *co. 1* and *ad 7* illuminates both the power and the principled boundaries of geometric formalization in GEOMETOR. Within *geometor.model*, points and unit segments function exactly as Aquinas describes *punctus lineae* and *unitas numeri*: not as isolated metaphysical substances, but as generative principles intrinsic to the constructive system of lines, circles, and algebraic ratios. Equally significant is Aquinas's explanation in *ad 7* of why mathematics excludes efficient and final causality in favor of pure formal necessity: in a constructive DAG within *geometor.elements* or *geometor.model*, ancestral dependencies represent timeless formal entailments rather than physical forces in time, even though the beauty and proportional harmony uncovered by *geometor.divine* invite philosophical reflection beyond pure geometry toward natural and metaphysical order.
