Commentary & Notes — How Mathematical Species Are Defined Without Sensible Matter Yet Not Without Common Intelligible Matter
============================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

In *Metaphysics* Z.11 (1036a26–1037b7), Aquinas tracks :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's polemic against two wings of the Old Academy: Platonists who collapsed mathematical objects directly into Ideal Numbers (identifying the straight line itself with the Dyad, probably Xenocrates) and those—including :person:`Plato <plato>` himself and Speusippus—who posited *mathematica media* between Ideal Numbers and sensible bodies while still claiming the Dyad is the formal *species* of the line (Marietti nn. 1509–1511). Aquinas also identifies 'Socrates the Younger' (1036b25; cf. Plato's *Theaetetus* 147d and *Statesman* 257c) with Plato's literary persona of Socrates, diagnosing why his 'parable' misleads: because a circle can exist without bronze, Plato inferred that human nature can exist and be defined without flesh and bone, and academic hyper-abstractionists inferred further that triangles and circles can be defined without continuous lines! Aquinas restores hylomorphic proportion: lines stand to the triangle in :term:`common intelligible matter <materia-intelligibilis-communis>` just as flesh and bone stand to man in common :term:`sensible matter <materia-sensibilis>`.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Lecture 11 defends the irreducibility of geometric continuity against arithmetic reductionism—a foundational principle of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements*, which treats continuous magnitude (Books I–VI, X–XIII) and discrete multitude (Books VII–IX) in distinct axiomatic books rather than reducing lines to pairs of points or triangles to the triad. In Marietti nn. 1508–1509, Aquinas notes that removing the continuous line from the definition of the triangle (bounded by three straight lines, *Elements* I, Def. 19) or the circle (bounded by one curved line, *Elements* I, Def. 15) leaves nothing save bare three-ness or oneness, obliterating spatial geometry altogether. Furthermore, in Marietti nn. 1520–1522 and 1529, Aquinas resolves why semicircles (*hemicycla*) never enter the definition of the universal circle even though letters enter the definition of a syllable: semicircles arise only by partitioning an individual circle's continuous extension, whereas :term:`common intelligible matter <materia-intelligibilis-communis>` (a plane figure bounded by one line equidistant from a center) is essential to the universal species itself.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

For :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` (*De Noetica Geometriae*, Caput II and Caput IV), *Metaphysics* VII, lect. 11 delivers the decisive refutation of modern pure Hilbertian formalism avant la lettre. Just as the ancient Platonists sought to purge the :term:`continuum <continuum>` (*linea vel superficies*) from the essence of geometric figures so that only abstract numerical or relational multiplicities remained—with the fatal consequence remarked by Aquinas in Marietti n. 1512 that a triangle (three lines), a syllogism (three terms), and three-dimensional space would become formally isomorphic—modern formalist axiomatics replaces intuitive spatial content with uninterpreted 'transcendent terms' (*a*, *b*, *c*) satisfying purely syntactic postulates. Hoenen shows with Aquinas that while symbolic logic (*logica docens*) legitimately studies formal structures in abstraction, geometry itself cannot dispense with *materia intelligibilis communis*—the intuitively grasped continuum—without ceasing to be geometry.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's critique of reducing geometric figures to bare numerical counts illuminates a core architectural distinction in GEOMETOR between purely combinatorial graph structures and genuine constructive geometry in *geometor.model*. An abstract incidence triple of three elements could equally represent three terms in a syllogism or three nodes in a tree (*geometor.trees*), losing every metric and proportional invariant of Euclidean space. Because *geometor.model* embeds lines and circles within exact continuous algebraic geometry—retaining :term:`common intelligible matter <materia-intelligibilis-communis>` through continuous quadratic and linear equations over symbolic coordinates—geometric figures preserve their intrinsic spatial curvature, incidence, and harmonic cross-ratios (*geometor.pappus*) rather than degenerating into uninterpreted combinatorial tokens.
