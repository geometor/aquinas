Commentary & Notes — On the Division of Per Se Being into Ten Categories and Being Signified by Propositional Judgment
======================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Aquinas's exposition of *Metaphysics* Δ.7 (1017a7–b9) transforms :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's compact lexical entry into a foundational treatise on analogical predication and the architecture of the categories (paralleled in *In III Physicorum*, lect. 5, in Part IV of this Codex). Against Parmenidean monism and Platonic univocity, Aquinas invokes *Metaphysics* B.3 (998b22) to prove that *ens* cannot be a genus, deriving the ten categories systematically from the triple relation of predicate to subject: essential identity (substance), intrinsic inherence consequent upon matter (quantity), upon form (quality), or toward another (relation), and extrinsic denomination by measure or causal principle/term. Equally decisive is his explicit refutation of :person:`Avicenna <avicenna>` (*Liber de Philosophia Prima* III.2–4), who treated concrete accidental predicates like *album* as signifying substance primarily and quality secondarily; Aquinas insists that *album* primarily signifies quality in an accidental mode (*esse est inesse*), preserving the integrity of the nine accidental categories—chief among them mathematical quantity—as genuine modes of *ens per se* rather than collapsing them into *ens per accidens*.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Two canonical Euclidean paradigms anchor Lecture 9's distinctions of propositional truth and potentiality. In Marietti n. 895, illustrating how *non esse* signifies the falsity of a proposition, Aquinas explicates :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's famous example (1017a34–35): to say that the diagonal of a square is *not* incommensurable with its side is to assert what is false—alluding to the Pythagorean-Euclidean proof (preserved in *Elements* X, Appendix 27, and implied by *Elements* I.47 and X.9) that the ratio of diagonal to side (√2 : 1) admits no common unit measure. Immediately afterward, in Marietti n. 897, illustrating *ens in potentia* across the categories, Aquinas cites the midpoint of a line (*medium lineae*) as existing potentially within the undivided line (*Elements* I.10 bisects a given finite straight line in act), noting explicitly that Aristotle places the line provisionally among substances in view of the Pythagorean-Platonic thesis not yet refuted in Book V.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In the Appendix to *De Noetica Geometriae* (1954) and throughout *La théorie du jugement d'après St. Thomas d'Aquin* (1946/1953), Peter :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` S.J. makes *In V Metaphysicorum*, lect. 9 (especially Marietti nn. 890, 895–896) the cornerstone of his distinction between the two operations of the intellect and the analogical diversity of *esse*. Hoenen highlights Aquinas's guiding principle—*quoties ens dicitur, toties esse significatur*—showing that diverse *modi essendi* (substantial subsistence, quantitative extension, qualitative intensity, fluent motion) originate from the quidditative natures and relations affirmed in judgment. Furthermore, by contrasting real categorical *esse* (cause) with propositional *est* as the copula of truth (effect, Marietti nn. 895–896), Aquinas provides the exact textual warrant for Hoenen's and Bernard :person:`Lonergan <lonergan>`\ 's (*Verbum*, Ch. 2) insistence that judgment is not a mere juxtaposition of concepts, but a reflective intellectual posit affirming (or denying, as in diagonal commensurability) the correspondence between mental synthesis and objective necessity.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's dual analysis of diagonal incommensurability and the potential midpoint of a segment speaks directly to the symbolic foundations of *geometor.model*. In floating-point dynamic geometry software, commensurability and incommensurability are blurred into indistinguishable decimal approximations, obliterating the exact modal boundary that Aquinas highlights in Marietti n. 895. Because *geometor.model* represents every point, line, and circle in exact SymPy algebraic fields, the irrationality of √2 in a unit square or the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 in a golden section is preserved as an exact polynomial identity, allowing propositional queries over collinearity, concurrency, and commensurability to evaluate with strict Boolean truth. Likewise, before a bisecting circle-pair construction is executed in *geometor.model*, the midpoint of a segment exists only virtually—in potency—until explicit constructive steps reduce that point into actual algebraic coordinates within the model's directed acyclic graph.
