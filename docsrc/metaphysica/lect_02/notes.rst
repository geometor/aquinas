Commentary & Notes — On Parts of the Form vs. Parts of Matter: Bronze Circle and Mathematical Circle in Intelligible Matter
===========================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Expositing *Metaphysics* Z.10 (1034b20–1036a25), Aquinas brings crisp scholastic order to one of the most intricate chapters in the Aristotelian corpus. He introduces a fourfold distinction—form (*forma*), universal species (*quod quid erat esse* embracing common matter), concrete singular composite (*simul totum* in individual matter), and material parts—to resolve when parts precede or follow the whole. Most remarkably, in Marietti nn. 1494–1496, Aquinas harmonizes :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's reference to singular mathematical circles (*circuli mathematici*) with *De Anima* III.5's enigmatic remark that the *intellectus passivus* is corruptible: mathematical singulars are grasped apart from external sensation by pure *phantasia* (here called *intellectus* or *intelligentia* by extension), proving against Platonic formalism that imagination supplies an inner spatial field (*materia intelligibilis*) capable of individuating multiple congruent lines and concentric or non-concentric circles of the exact same species.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Every geometric example in Lecture 10 illuminates the logical ordering of definitions in :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements* Book I. Aquinas's observation (Marietti n. 1483) that the acute angle is quantitatively part of the right angle yet posterior in definition reproduces *Elements* Book I, Definitions 10–12 verbatim: Euclid first defines the right angle via the equality of adjacent angles formed by a perpendicular straight line, and only subsequently defines the obtuse angle as greater than a right angle and the acute angle as *less than a right angle* (*angulus minor recto*). Similarly, *Elements* Book I, Definitions 15–18 define the circle first through the equidistance of its bounding line from one interior point, and only then define the diameter and the semicircle (*hemicyclium*) through the circle. Finally, Aquinas's appeal (Marietti n. 1494) to 'several equal lines and several similar figures' (*plures lineae aequales, et plures figurae similes*) captures the pervasive practice of Euclidean congruence and similarity proofs (Books I and VI), which compare two numerically distinct triangles or circles instantiating one identical universal definition.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

:person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` explicitly cites *In VII Metaphysicorum*, lect. 10, Marietti n. 1496 alongside *Super Boetium De Trinitate* q. 5, a. 3, ad 3 in *De Noetica Geometriae*, Caput IV (§ 8, n. 2), to establish the ontological ground of geometric individuation and congruence. Matter is a principle of numerical multiplicity solely through presupposed dimensive quantity, upon whose removal substance remains indivisible. Because pure continuous extension (*continuum vel superficies vel corpus*) retains dimensive parts even when all sensible qualities (bronze, wood, temperature, color) are abstracted away, the geometer's imagination possesses an authentic *materia intelligibilis* in which numerically distinct figures of identical species can be constructed, superposed, and inspected. As Hoenen emphasizes, when a particular imagined circle 'recedes from actual inspection' (*abeuntes ab actu*, Marietti n. 1495), only the universal *ratio* remains in habitual intellect, explaining why actual geometric reasoning must continually reactivate determinate spatial :term:`phantasms <phantasma>`.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Lecture 10's distinction between the universal definition of the circle and plural *circuli mathematici* individuated in *materia intelligibilis* mirrors the exact object model of *geometor.model* and *geometor.explorer*. In *geometor.model*, the universal algebraic form of a circle—the locus of points at a fixed squared radius *r²* from a center (*x₀*, *y₀*)—is instantiated as distinct symbolic *Circle* objects placed at distinct positions in the coordinate plane. Just as Aquinas proves in Marietti n. 1494 that geometry requires plural equal segments and plural circles of the same species to conduct demonstrations, constructive geometry in *geometor.model* begins from two initial points defining a unit segment and immediately instantiates two distinct congruent circles centered at each endpoint (:person:`Euclid <euclid>` I.1), while *geometor.explorer* renders those exact intelligible singulars on an SVG canvas for visual inspection.
