Commentary & Notes — On Positional Directions in Natural Bodies and How They Are Considered by Position in Mathematical Figures
===============================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Aquinas comments here on the first half of *De Caelo* II.2 (284b6–285b8), where :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` critiques the Pythagorean attribution of *right* and *left* to the cosmos (cf. *Metaphysics* I.5, 986a22–26, the ten-column *systoichia* of principles) by importing the biological mechanics of *De Incessu Animalium* (*On the Progression of Animals*, chs. 2–5, 704b12–706b16) and *De Anima* II.4 / III.9–10. Following :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` (*In De Caelo*, ed. Heiberg, 383–393), Aquinas distinguishes three senses of dimensional priority—by dimensional magnitude (length, breadth, depth), by genetic construction (line, surface, body), and by causal origin of vital motion (nutritive *above*, sensory *before*, locomotive *right*)—and highlights the pivotal philosophical contrast in nn. 6–7 (285a1–10): whereas organisms possess intrinsic functional chirality and polarity independent of external orientation, inanimate bodies and geometric objects lack internal directional principles and receive *above* and *below*, *right* and *left*, and *before* and *behind* solely *quoad nos*—including enantiomorphic inversion in specular reflection (*in speculo*).

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

In :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's Greek text at *De Caelo* II.2, 285a1–4 (reflected in Aquinas's analysis of objects lacking an intrinsic principle of motion in nn. 6–7 and geometric generation in n. 11), triangles and squares are the classic exemplars of inanimate/mathematical objects whose *above*, *below*, *right*, and *left* are assigned purely by positional convention (*thesei* / *per comparationem ad nos*) and reverse whenever the figure is rotated. This principle is fundamental to :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements*: in Book I Definitions 20–22 and Propositions 4, 8, and 26, a triangle's 'base' (*basis*), 'vertex' (*koruphe*), 'left side,' and 'right side' are never intrinsic properties of the figure itself, as Euclid explicitly signals in I.4 and I.5 by choosing whichever side serves the proof as base. Even more remarkably, Aquinas's third relational mode in n. 7—*contraria positio* in a mirror image (*imago quae resultat in speculo*), where right and left interchange while preserving figure (*conveniunt in figura*, n. 9)—identifies exact enantiomorphic (chiral) congruence, the very relation tested when Euclid applies inversely oriented congruent triangles in *Elements* I.5 (Pons Asinorum) without altering metric equality.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In *De Noetica Geometriae* (Chapter IV, § 2, fn. 9, citing *In II De Caelo*, lect. 2, n. 11 on how length precedes breadth because according to geometers a moving line generates a surface, and Chapter IV, § 4.2, pp. 183–205 on the notion of *direction* and orientation), Peter :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` S.J. develops Aquinas's distinction between intrinsic vital polarity and relational geometric position into a rigorous theory of spatial isotropy and relative orientation. Pure geometric extension (*materia intelligibilis*) is completely homogeneous and isotropic: no point or axis possesses an intrinsic *above* or *right* prior to a chosen reference line. Yet once a single directed line *a* passing through a point *P* in a plane is posited, two opposite senses of angular divergence ('on the right' and 'on the left' relative to *a*, p. 194) immediately become objectively distinguishable as relational *differences in site alone*. Thus Hoenen shows how Aquinas's *per comparationem ad nos* in *De Caelo* II.2 translates mathematically into frame-relative orientation and reflection symmetry, preserving Euclidean invariance across every rotation of the coordinate frame.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's demonstration in *In II De Caelo*, lect. 2 that geometric figures possess no absolute *up*, *down*, *left*, or *right* in themselves—acquiring directional labels solely relative to an adopted reference frame and inverting chirality under mirror reflection—captures a foundational principle of symbolic modeling in ``geometor.model`` and ``geometor.divine``. Although ``geometor.model`` typically seeds a canonical construction with two initial points along a horizontal axis at ``(0, 0)`` and ``(1, 0)`` to fix a reference orientation (*quoad nos*), the emergent golden sections, harmonic ranges, and equilateral or pentagonal symmetries detected by ``geometor.divine`` are completely invariant under rigid rotation, translation, and mirror reflection (*in speculo*). Moreover, Aquinas's observation in nn. 9 and 13 that right and left agree in *figure* (*conveniunt in figura*) while reversing orientation anticipates bilateral and chiral symmetry analysis in ``geometor.phyllotaxis`` and ``geometor.seer``, where parastichy spirals (clockwise versus counterclockwise Fibonacci numbers) and visual grid transformations exhibit exact mirror duality.
