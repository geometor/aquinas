Commentary & Notes — How the Mathematician Differs from the Natural Philosopher and Why There Is No Falsehood in Those Who Abstract
===================================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Aquinas's exposition comments on :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's *Physics* II.2 (193b22–194a12), the locus classicus distinguishing natural philosophy (*physike*) from mathematics against Platonic Realism. Where Aristotle tersely remarks that the mathematician separates (*chorizei*) surfaces, volumes, lengths, and points in thought from motion without generating falsehood (*oude ginetai pseudos chorizonton*, 193b35—rendered in the Moerbeke-vetus tradition as *neque fit mendacium abstrahentium*), Aquinas supplies a full metaphysical justification drawn from *Metaphysics* VII.10–11 (1036a9–12) and :person:`Averroes <averroes>`\ 's *Long Commentary on the Physics* (II, comm. 18–20). By establishing the ontological order in which accidents accrue to corporeal substance—dimensive quantity first, then sensible qualities, then active and passive powers and local or qualitative motions—Aquinas explains *why* mathematical abstraction is non-deceptive while Platonic separation of natural forms (flesh, bone, man) fails. Likewise, Aquinas's analysis of *the snub* (*to simon*, 194a1–7) versus *the curved* (*to koilon*) and of the *ta physikotera ton mathematon* (194a7–12: optics, harmonics, astronomy) harmonizes Aristotle's *Physics* II.2 with *Posterior Analytics* I.7–13 (75b14–17, 78b34–79a16) on subalternated sciences and *De Caelo* II.14 (297a8–298a20) on the physical versus astronomical proofs of the earth's sphericity, echoing :person:`Simplicius <simplicius>` (*In Phys.*, ed. Diels, 290–293) who cites Geminus and Posidonius on the methodological boundary between causal physics and saving-the-phenomena mathematical astronomy.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

In :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements*, Book I Definitions 1–7 and Book XI Definitions 1–2 define point, line, straight line, surface, plane surface, and solid without a single reference to weight, temperature, optical medium, or physical resistance. Aquinas's commentary on *Physics* II.3 provides the exact epistemological warrant for Euclid's procedure: the Euclidean line is ontological reality—the very quantitative limit existing in natural bodies—considered apart from :term:`sensible matter <materia-sensibilis>` (*linea naturalis abstracte considerata*). Moreover, Aquinas's distinction between pure geometry and the *sciences intermediate between mathematics and physics* directly illuminates the Euclidean corpus itself, where pure planar and stereometric theorems in the *Elements* are subalternated in Euclid's *Optics* and *Catoptrics* (applying abstract straight lines and equal angles to visual rays, *lineae visuales*) and in the Euclidean *Sectio Canonis* (applying arithmetic ratios of numbers to consonant musical intervals). Even the dual demonstration of terrestrial sphericity in n. 9 illustrates how Euclidean circular and spherical geometry furnishes formal *propter quid* middle terms (lunar shadow cross-sections and angular stellar altitudes) for phenomena grounded physically in elemental gravitation.

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In *De Noetica Geometriae* (Chapter II, § 7, linking *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 85, a. 1, ad 2 directly with *In II Phys.*, lect. 3, n. 5, and Chapter III, § 2), Peter :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` S.J. makes Aquinas's ordering of quantity prior to sensible qualities the linchpin of geometric exactitude and necessity. Against John Stuart Mill's empiricist reduction of geometric figures to inexact copies of sense impressions (*minimum visibile*) and J. Wellstein's voluntarist claim that exact boundaries require an arbitrary act of the will, Hoenen argues that intellectual abstraction inspecting the :term:`phantasm <phantasma>` grasps extended substance purely *as extended*—that is, clothed in *intelligible matter* while prescinding from all sensible qualities. Because *there is no falsehood in those who abstract*, the intellect sees with apodictic certainty that three-dimensional extension *per se* admits exact indivisible boundaries (surfaces, lines, and points) even if microscopic physical surfaces in sensible nature are violently wrinkled. Thus *In II Phys.*, lect. 3 grounds Hoenen's central thesis that mathematical existence is neither a Platonic fiction nor a formalist postulate, but an objective necessity apprehended in real extension.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

For the GEOMETOR initiative, Aquinas's principle that *there is no falsehood in those who abstract* defines the philosophical divide between exact symbolic geometry and numerical floating-point approximation. When ``geometor.model`` represents points, lines, and circles through exact algebraic expressions in SymPy—preserving quadratic and higher polynomial surds such as the square root of five rather than truncating coordinates to finite IEEE-754 floats or rasterized pixels—it enacts Aquinas's intellectual abstraction from :term:`sensible matter <materia-sensibilis>` (physical measurement noise and display resolution) while retaining *intelligible matter* (the continuous algebraic plane in which distinct congruent figures are individuated by coordinate position). Equally, Aquinas's account of the *sciences intermediate between mathematics and physics* models the relationship within the GEOMETOR ecosystem between pure symbolic construction in ``geometor.model`` and ``geometor.elements`` on the one hand, and applied structural analysis in ``geometor.phyllotaxis`` or visual SVG rendering in ``geometor.explorer`` on the other, where pure geometric proportions and golden-ratio invariants are re-applied to botanical growth patterns and sensible visual diagrams.
