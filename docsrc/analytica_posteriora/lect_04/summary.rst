Summary — What Unqualified Scientific Knowledge Is and How Demonstration Proceeds from Primary, True, Immediate Causes
======================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Analytical Synopsis & Core Theses of Lib. I, lect. 4*

Argument Summary
----------------

Beginning the treatise on the demonstrative syllogism proper, Aquinas explains why :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` constructs two linked definitions of demonstration: a formal-teleological definition taken from its end (*to know scientifically*, *scire*), which serves as the middle term proving its material definition taken from the required character of its premises. Setting aside sophistical knowing *per accidens* (such as knowing Coriscus approaching), Aquinas expounds Aristotle's three conditions for *knowing unqualifiedly* (*scire simpliciter*): knowing the cause of the thing, knowing its actual application as the cause of that effect, and knowing with certitude that it cannot be otherwise. Demonstration is accordingly defined as a *scientific syllogism* (*syllogismus scientialis*)—one whose very possession constitutes scientific knowledge—which must therefore proceed from premises that are *true*, *primary*, *immediate* (lacking any prior middle term), *better known* than, *prior* to, and *causes* of the conclusion.

Manifesting each material condition, Aquinas illustrates the impossibility of knowing falsehoods through :person:`Euclid <euclid>` Book X: because *to be* and *to be true* are convertible, one cannot scientifically know that the diagonal of a square is commensurable (*symmetrum*) with its side, since they necessarily lack any common numerical measure. Finally, Aquinas harmonizes the *Posterior Analytics* thesis that demonstrative premises are prior *according to nature* rather than *in relation to us* (where sensible singulars come first) with *Physics* I: in natural philosophy, material essences are hidden from us so we frequently argue *a posteriori* from sensible effects, whereas in mathematics, owing to abstraction from :term:`sensible matter <materia-sensibilis>`, demonstrations proceed purely from formal principles in which what is better known *in relation to us* is simultaneously better known *unqualifiedly* and *according to nature*.

Key Theses
----------

* Unqualified scientific knowledge (*scire simpliciter*) requires knowing the cause, its actual causal nexus with the effect, and the impossibility of the truth being otherwise.
* Because *being* and *being true* are convertible, falsehoods—such as the commensurability of the square's diagonal with its side (*Elements* X)—cannot be scientifically known, nor can science arise from false or unproved premises.
* Demonstrative premises must be primary, immediate, causal, and naturally prior to and better known than the conclusion.
* In mathematics, unlike natural philosophy, abstraction from sensible matter ensures that formal principles better known *in relation to us* are simultaneously better known *unqualifiedly* and *according to nature*.
