Commentary & Notes — On Democritus's Account of Motion and How Geometers Imagine a Moving Point Describing a Line
=================================================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Aristotelian Sources, Euclidean Counterparts, Hoenen's Noetics, and GEOMETOR Significance*

Aristotelian & Scholastic Foundations
-------------------------------------

Aquinas comments here on :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's *De Anima* I.4 (408b32–409b18), continuing the critique of kinetic theories of the soul begun at I.3 (406b15–25, where :person:`Democritus <democritus>`\ 's spherical fire-atoms and :person:`Plato <plato>`\ 's *Timaeus* circles were first paired). Xenocrates, scholarch of the Old Academy after Speusippus, sought to synthesize Platonic mathematical metaphysics with kinetic psychology by defining the soul as 'number moving itself' (ἀριθμὸς κινῶν ἑαυτόν). Following Aristotle's cross-references to *Physics* VI (an indivisible cannot move *per se*, nor compose a :term:`continuum <continuum>`) and *Physics* VIII (every self-mover divides into an unmoved or moving part and a moved part), Aquinas exposes a fatal category mistake: once arithmetic units are localized inside an animate body, they acquire spatial position (*positio* / θέσις) and become indistinguishable either from geometric points or from Democritus's corpuscular atoms, rendering them incapable of either self-motion or biological formal causality.

Euclidean & Classical Geometric Counterparts
--------------------------------------------

Paragraph 170 directly engages the foundational tension in Greek geometry between static essential definitions and genetic or kinematic descriptions. In :person:`Euclid <euclid>`\ 's *Elements*, Book I, Definitions 1–4 define the point ('that which has no part'), line ('breadthless length'), extremities of a line, and straight line purely statically by boundaries and dimensional privation, without invoking motion; yet Postulates 1–3 ('to draw a straight line from any point to any point,' 'to produce a finite straight line continuously,' 'to describe a circle with any center and distance') and Book XI's solid revolutions (spheres, cones, and cylinders generated by rotating semicircles, triangles, and rectangles) rely on kinematic construction. Proclus (*In Euclidem*, p. 97 and p. 185, citing Geminus) preserves the Academic characterization of the line as the 'flux of a point' (ῥύσις τοῦ σημείου) and the straight line as an 'even and undeviating flux.' Aquinas's exposition clarifies why Euclid excluded motion from Book I's essential definitions of point and line while retaining constructive postulates: motion belongs to the geometer's imaginative generation of figures, whereas ontological priority in the :term:`continuum <continuum>` proceeds from solid body down to bounding surface, line, and point (no. 174).

Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae & Judgment Theory
------------------------------------------------

In *De Noetica Geometriae*, Chapter IV, § 2 (with footnotes 8 and 9 citing *In I De Anima*, lect. 11, Pirotta no. 170 alongside *In De Caelo* I, lect. 2, no. 9 and *In Physicorum* IV, lect. 18, no. 4), Peter :person:`Hoenen <hoenen>` S.J. highlights Aquinas's precise epistemological diagnosis: whereas :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`\ 's text at 409a4 simply says 'they say' (φασι) that a moved point makes a line—prompting Trendelenburg to wonder whether the Pythagoreans or all geometers were meant—Aquinas identifies the ontological thesis as Platonist here (no. 170) while elsewhere attributing the idiom to 'geometers imagining (*imaginantes*) that a moved point makes a line.' Hoenen uses this distinction to resolve modern objections (such as Bertrand :person:`Russell <russell>`\ 's and Hermann von Helmholtz's critiques of superposition and kinematic geometry): geometry does not treat physical efficient causality or temporal velocity, which belong to physics, but legitimately employs imagined continuous motion as a genetic *description* (not a strict definition) grounded in the homogeneity of spatial extension as *intelligible matter* and principle of individuation, from which both passive rigid-body mobility and constant direction necessarily flow.

GEOMETOR Constructive & Symbolic Significance
---------------------------------------------

Aquinas's distinction between the arithmetic unit lacking position, the geometric point as a 'unit having position' (*unitas positionem habens*), and the kinematic imagination of a point tracing a line illuminates the core architectural duality of GEOMETOR. Within ``geometor.model``, points are not floating-point pixels or Democritean corpuscles of small breadth, but exact algebraic indivisibles endowed with symbolic coordinates—literally exact units possessing position within an algebraic field over the rationals and quadratic surds. Meanwhile, when ``geometor.explorer`` animates a compass sweep or straightedge extension across the SVG canvas, it enacts precisely the geometer's kinematic imagination described in Pirotta no. 170: the visual sweep does not *constitute* the algebraic essence of the line or circle, which exists timelessly in polynomial incidence relations, yet it renders the genetic dependency graph of the construction transparent to human intuition.
