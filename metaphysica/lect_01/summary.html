Summary — On the Division of Per Se Being into Ten Categories and Being Signified by Propositional Judgment¶

Analytical Synopsis & Core Theses of Metaph. V, lect. 9

Argument Summary¶

Commenting on Aristotle‘s philosophical lexicon (Metaphysics Δ.7, 1017a7–b9), Aquinas distinguishes ens per accidens (accidental predication across a common subject) from ens per se, which he subdivides into three fundamental orders: first, extramental ens perfectum contracted proportionally into the ten categories according to diverse modes of predicating that track diverse modes of being (correcting Avicenna‘s claim that accidental terms primarily signify substance); second, intramental esse signified by the verbal copula of propositional composition and division, which expresses the truth of a statement as effect to extramental cause (illustrated by privations like blindness and the negation of the diagonal’s commensurability with the side of a square); and third, the division of all categories by potency and act, illustrated by the statue of Mercury in stone and the midpoint latent in potency within a continuous line.

Key Theses¶

  • Being (ens) cannot be a genus contracted by extrinsic differences, because nothing lies outside being; instead, it contracts into the ten categories proportionally as modi praedicandi follow real modi essendi.

  • Quantity inheres in substance per se and absolutely as consequent upon matter, whereas quality follows upon form and relation regards another.

  • Propositional esse formed by the composing and dividing intellect signifies that a statement is true (quia aliquod dictum est verum), extending even to privations and mathematical impossibilities such as the incommensurability of the square’s diagonal.

  • Every category is divided by act and potency, just as every part of a continuum—such as the midpoint of a line—exists potentially in the whole prior to actual division.
